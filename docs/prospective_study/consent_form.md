### 1. Purpose of the Study

You are being invited to participate in a research study. The purpose of this study is to investigate how electrical brain activity (measured using electroencephalography, or EEG) changes when people perform mentally demanding arithmetic tasks. We aim to understand whether specific brain wave patterns can predict cognitive workload and performance. This research will contribute to a science fair project (ISEF-affiliated) and may be submitted for publication in a scientific journal.

### 2. Procedures

If you agree to participate, you will be asked to attend a single laboratory session lasting approximately 60 minutes. The session will involve the following:

- **EEG Setup (20 minutes):** A soft elastic cap with small metal sensors (electrodes) will be placed on your head. A conductive gel will be applied to each sensor site to improve signal quality. The gel is water-soluble and will be washed out after the session.
- **Rest Recording (5 minutes):** You will sit quietly with your eyes open, looking at a fixation cross on a computer screen.
- **Practice (2 minutes):** You will perform a brief practice version of the arithmetic task.
- **Arithmetic Task (15 minutes, three 5-minute blocks):** You will perform serial subtraction problems (e.g., counting backward by 7 from 1000). The difficulty will adjust automatically based on your performance. After each block, you will complete a short computerized questionnaire (NASA-TLX) about your perceived workload.
- **Post-Task Rest (5 minutes):** You will again sit quietly with your eyes open.
- **Debriefing (5 minutes):** The experimenter will explain the study's goals and answer any questions.

During the session, we will record your brain activity (EEG), your verbal responses (audio recorded to measure response timing), and your questionnaire responses.

### 3. Risks and Discomforts

This study involves **minimal risk**. EEG is a non-invasive technique that only records your brain's natural electrical activity — no electrical current is passed through your head.

Potential discomforts include:

- **Scalp discomfort or skin irritation:** The conductive gel or gentle skin preparation may cause mild redness or itching at electrode sites. This typically resolves within a few hours. We use hypoallergenic gel and minimal skin abrasion.
- **Fatigue:** The 60-minute session may cause mild mental fatigue. You may request a break at any time.
- **Mild frustration:** The arithmetic task may be challenging. Your performance does not affect your compensation, and you may stop at any time.

If you experience any unexpected discomfort or distress, please inform the researcher immediately.

### 4. Benefits

There are no direct personal benefits to you for participating. However, your participation will contribute to scientific knowledge about brain function during cognitive tasks, and the results may ultimately help improve brain–computer interface technologies and cognitive workload assessment methods.

### 5. Confidentiality

All information collected during this study will be kept confidential to the extent permitted by law.

- **Data Storage:** Your data will be stored under a numeric code (e.g., ISEF_042), not your name. A separate, encrypted linking file connecting your name to your code will be stored on a password-protected institutional computer accessible only to the research team.
- **De-identification:** Before any data is shared or published, all potentially identifying information will be removed. Voice recordings will be processed to extract response timing and then deleted, unless you separately consent to voice data sharing.
- **Data Sharing:** De-identified, anonymized EEG data and behavioral responses may be shared on public scientific databases (e.g., OpenNeuro) to allow other researchers to verify and build on our findings. No identifying information will be included.
- **Publications:** Results may be presented at science fairs (e.g., ISEF), conferences, or published in scientific journals. Individual participants will not be identifiable in any publication.

### 6. Voluntary Participation and Right to Withdraw

Your participation in this study is **completely voluntary**. You may choose not to participate or may withdraw at any time, for any reason, without penalty or loss of benefits to which you are otherwise entitled. If you withdraw, you may request that your data be destroyed and not used in the analysis.

If you withdraw after EEG setup but before completing the task, you will receive a prorated $10 gift card. If you withdraw before EEG setup, you will not receive compensation.

### 7. Compensation

You will receive a **$20 gift card** for completing the full session. Please allow 5 minutes after the session for processing.
